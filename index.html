How We Can Work Together

Every engagement is built around one simple idea: make delivery reliable, make performance measurable, and make leadership calmer. The levers are systems, behaviour, and operating rhythm – not motivational speeches.

Fractional Talent Ops Retainer

Part-time senior leadership for founders who need a Head of Talent/Delivery but are not ready for a full-time executive.

  • 10–20 hours per week over 3–12 months, embedded with your leadership team.
  • Owns the talent operations roadmap: role clarity, headcount planning, recruiter capacity, and delivery risks.
  • Builds and runs an operating cadence: weekly delivery reviews, monthly performance deep-dives, and clear decision forums.
  • Coaches team leads so they can eventually run the system without fractional support.

This is for you if you feel like the de facto Head of Delivery and want to step out of the day-to-day without losing control.

60–90 Day Delivery Stabilisation Sprints

Intensive, time-bound projects to fix one or two critical failure points in your recruitment engine.

  • Delivery Stabilisation: Map your end-to-end process, identify bottlenecks, and redesign handoffs so work flows reliably.
  • Recruiter Productivity Reset: Clarify expectations, rebuild dashboards, and install a realistic activity/quality rhythm.
  • Hiring Workflow Redesign: Standardise intake, screening, and selection so every hire follows one consistent, measurable path.
  • Clear before/after metrics agreed upfront (e.g. time-to-fill, drop-off, interview-to-offer ratios, fill rate by team).

Ideal when things feel “messy but fixable” and you want tangible progress in 1–3 months before considering a longer retainer.

Diagnostics, Audits & Workshops

Short, sharp interventions to create shared reality and unblock decisions.

  • Talent Ops Diagnostic: A structured review of your funnel, roles, tools, and metrics, with a prioritised action plan.
  • Accountability & Role Clarity Workshops: Get everyone on the same page about who owns what, and where decisions actually sit.
  • Leadership & Behavioural Audits: Understand the patterns (avoidance, firefighting, perfectionism) that keep problems in place.
  • Often used as a low-risk way to start before a sprint or retainer.

The output is not a slide deck to file away; it is a concrete, sequenced list of moves the team can start on immediately.

How It Works

The process is deliberately simple. The goal is to move from “vague pain and overwhelm” to “clear problems, clear plan, clear ownership” in a matter of weeks, not quarters.

1. Delivery & Talent Ops Scan

  • 60–90 minute conversation to understand your firm (niche, team size, client mix) and where delivery is breaking down.
  • High-level review of your current process, roles, and basic metrics (if they exist).
  • Clarify what “good” would look like three months from now, in plain language.

Outcome: A short summary of the 3–5 most important issues and whether a sprint, a retainer, or a simple diagnostic is the best first move.

2. Diagnose & Design

  • Map the end-to-end journey from client demand to filled role, including handoffs and decision points.
  • Identify bottlenecks, failure modes, and behaviour patterns that are keeping problems in place.
  • Co-design a small number of concrete interventions: changes to roles, workflows, meetings, and metrics.

Outcome: A practical, prioritised plan for the next 60–90 days, grounded in your reality rather than a generic maturity model.

3. Embed & Coach

  • Work alongside you and your leads to implement the changes with as little disruption as possible.
  • Install a simple operating cadence: weekly reviews that look at the same metrics in the same way.
  • Coach managers on having clear, adult conversations about performance and accountability.

Outcome: New ways of working that actually stick because they make life easier for the people running them.

4. Review & Decide What’s Next

  • Review progress against the agreed metrics and the “three-month picture” defined at the start.
  • Decide whether to close the engagement, extend for another cycle, or shift the focus.
  • Hand over tools, templates, and rhythms so your team can continue without external support.

Outcome: A clean exit or a conscious decision to deepen the work – not an open-ended, fuzzy arrangement.

What This Looks Like in Practice

Names and details are anonymised, but the situations are real. The common pattern: strong demand, overloaded founders, and teams trying to scale on informal habits.

01. Regional IT Staffing Firm (20 recruiters)

  • Context: Multi-country IT staffing firm, 20 recruiters, strong demand but uneven performance across offices.
  • Problem: 40% recruiter churn, unclear ownership between sales and delivery, frequent “fire drills” to save key accounts.
  • What we did:
    • Defined a simple role and decision-rights framework for sales, delivery leads, and recruiters.
    • Introduced a weekly delivery and risk review with a standard agenda and shared metrics.
    • Reworked recruiter scorecards to balance activity, quality, and client impact.
  • Outcomes (90 days):
    • Delivery variability reduced; on-time fulfilment up by roughly one-third.
    • Voluntary recruiter churn dropped from ~40% to ~15% annualised.
    • Founder stepped out of day-to-day firefighting on two major accounts.

02. Professional Services Agency (12-person team)

  • Context: Niche professional services firm with 3 anchor clients and an overextended founder managing both delivery and growth.
  • Problem: Hiring cycles creeping past 60 days, no clear owner for capacity planning, and inconsistent utilisation across the team.
  • What we did:
    • Built a simple headcount and capacity model tied to revenue and client commitments.
    • Clarified decision rights around who can greenlight roles, offers, and exceptions.
    • Designed a 60-day hiring and onboarding playbook with clear gates and timelines.
  • Outcomes (4–6 months):
    • Average hiring time reduced from ~60 days to about 30 days.
    • Founder recovered ~20 hours per week to focus on clients and strategy.
    • Revenue grew by just under 20% on the back of increased, predictable capacity.

03. Global Recruitment Team (35 recruiters, multi-market)

  • Context: Recruitment team spread across several regions, servicing global accounts with shared roles and overlapping responsibilities.
  • Problem: Handoff delays between sourcing, delivery, and account management; leaders lacked an agreed view of performance.
  • What we did:
    • Mapped the end-to-end process and rebuilt handoffs with explicit entry/exit criteria.
    • Introduced a common metrics set: time-to-shortlist, time-to-offer, fill rate, and drop-off points.
    • Coached team leads on running structured weekly reviews instead of ad-hoc check-ins.
  • Outcomes (3–4 months):
    • Handoff delays reduced by roughly 50% on key accounts.
    • Team-level productivity improved by just over 20% based on filled roles per recruiter.
    • Escalations from global clients reduced, and renewal conversations became calmer and more data-driven.
